Simulium nodosum Puri, 1933

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Simulium
Statusvalid
Other names in keysSimulium shirakii
DistributionIndia, China, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam (Takaoka et al. 2019) | Thailand (Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000; Takaoka & Choochote 2004). | India (type locality; Mukherjee et al. 2022) | Southern China; Myanmar (Chiang-Tung, 958 m); Thailand (Chiang Mai, Mae Hong Son, Kamphaeng Phet, 168-800+ m); Vietnam (Lam Dong 1,439 m, Nghe An 73 m, Vinh Phuc 283 m); Taiwan (Hualien: Guangfu 70 m, Ruisui 15 m, formerly S. shirakii) (Low et al. 2015; Srisuka et al. 2017; Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024)
ThailandThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 57) | Listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 189); collected with S. dentistylum in Ubon Ratchathani/Nakhon Si Thammarat (Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000 pp. 173-174). | Thailand: Doi Saket, Chiang Mai (733 m) (Low et al. 2015) | 301 mature larvae (1.5% of total), 15.5% stream occurrence (Srisuka et al. 2017) | Mae Hong Son (Tham Pla, 385 m) and Kamphaeng Phet (Tao Dam Waterfall, Khlonglan, 539 m), n=31 females (Aupalee et al. 2024)
ImportanceKnown to bite humans in Thailand (Choochote et al. 2005; Pramual et al. 2016) and incriminated as a natural vector of filariae including an Onchocerca species (Fukuda et al. 2003; Ishii et al. 2008; Takaoka et al. 2003; cited in Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 54). | Mammalophilic: bites humans and bovids across its range (incl. Thailand and Taiwan); natural vector of filarial nematodes (ruminant/zoonotic Onchocerca spp., including Onchocerca sp. Type I in Thailand) (Low et al. 2015; Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026 p. 12).
Diagnostic notesSimulium nobile species-group (Takaoka et al. 2019). | Simulium s.str. (nobile species-group). Female: simple claw, unvittate scutum, mid tarsal segments 2 and 3 entirely yellow. Male: scutum broadly silvery pruinose with transverse inverted-V-shaped black band, wide ventral plate broadened medially. Pupa: gill of 3 inflated tubes with rounded apex. Larva: dorsal abdominal protuberances, postgenal cleft reaching hypostomium, pharate pupal gill with 3 filaments (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 pp. 194-196). | S. nobile species-group. Low et al. (2015) synonymize S. shirakii Kono & Takahasi (Taiwan) with S. nodosum based on 4-gene DNA (COI+12S+16S+28S: 0-0.19% divergence vs 5.10-5.45% to S. nobile), identical fixed chromosomal inversions (IS-1, IIL-a, IIIL-b, IIIL-2; shared IIIL-1 polymorphism), morphology (female simple claws; pupal gill of 3 inflated horn-like tubes) and ecology/hosts. Wing centroid size 2.19 ± 0.12 mm (smallest among 7 Thai human-biters; range 1.95-2.40 mm); wing shape GM accuracy 96.77% (30/31); monophyletic in COI (max K2P 0.31% in Thailand; 0-2.97% across Vietnam/Thailand where PTP_ML/ASAP split 2 OTUs) (Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024).
