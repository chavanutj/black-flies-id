Simulium triglobus Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok, 1999

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Simulium
Statusvalid
DistributionThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019) | Thailand (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999; Takaoka & Choochote 2004).
ThailandThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 57) | Type locality: Tontong Waterfall (altitude 500 m), Doi Phuka National Park, Nan Province, northern Thailand (9.XII.1998, on fallen leaves in small 0.5-1.0 m stream, 19 C, with S. rufibasis; holotype female, paratypes 1 female, 1 pupa, 2 mature larvae) (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999 p. 505); listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 189).
Diagnostic notesSimulium multistriatum species-group (Takaoka et al. 2019). Male unknown (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 75). | Simulium s.str. (multistriatum species-group); described by Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok (1999 pp. 504-507). First species of Simuliidae known to possess three well-sclerotized spermathecae (1 large pear-shaped principal spermatheca on longer duct + 2 smaller oblong curved accessory spermathecae on shorter ducts, all with internal setae). Female (body 2.6 mm, wing 2.5 mm): 5-vittate scutum, simple claws, all femora entirely yellow (apex of hind femur slightly darkened; much paler than S. chaliowae and S. chainarongi), sensory vesicle 0.36x 3rd palpal segment, sternite 8 with 32-36 stout hairs/side, genital fork arms with wide plate-like anterior projection. Male unknown in 1999 and 2004. Pupa (2.8 mm): 8 short paired subequal gill filaments (1.0-0.6 mm, 140 deg angle, dorsalmost 1.3x ventralmost), thoracic trichomes more branched (4-5 branches mediodorsally) than S. chaliowae/S. chainarongi; corbicular shoe-shaped cocoon roughly woven anteriorly with large and small open spaces in anterior collar. Larva (5.4-6.0 mm): lacking abdominal protuberances; labral fan 56-60 rays; hypostomal teeth apically rounded; postgenal cleft rounded anteriorly, ca. 2.0x postgenal bridge; rectal gill 10-13 lobules/lobe; posterior circlet 112-134 rows of up to 22 hooklets (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999 pp. 504-507; Takaoka & Choochote 2004 pp. 194-197).
SourcesTakaoka et al. 2019, Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999, Takaoka & Choochote 2004
