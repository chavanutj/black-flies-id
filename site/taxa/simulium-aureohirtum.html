Simulium aureohirtum Brunetti, 1911

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Nevermannia
Statusvalid
DistributionIndia, Bhutan, China, Guam, Indonesia (Flores, Java, Kalimantan, Lombok, Maluku, Sulawesi, Sumatra, Timor), Japan, Malaysia, Nepal, Pakistan, Philippines, Sri Lanka, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam (Takaoka et al. 2019) | Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004, 2005). | India (Mukherjee et al. 2022) | Indonesia: Sumatra (West Sumatra, associated with S. kandisense and S. kotsuboyui) (Takaoka 2025) | Thailand (Srisuka et al. 2017) | Vietnam: Nghe An (Quy Chau, 80 m) and Lam Dong (Lac Duong, 1,443 m) (Putt et al. 2023)
ThailandThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 56) | Listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 189); Phu Hung, Muang District, Mukdahan Province, NE Thailand, 270 m, slow-flowing lowland stream, associated with S. oblongum (Takaoka & Choochote 2005 p. 40). | Thailand: 408 mature larvae (2.1% of total), 22.4% stream occurrence; found only during rainy season in central and eastern regions (Srisuka et al. 2017)
Diagnostic notesSimulium ruficorne species-group (Takaoka et al. 2019). | Nevermannia (ruficorne species-group). Female and male: antenna yellow or yellowish brown with at least 1st flagellar segment darkened; female hind tibia yellow on basal 1/2 with subbasal dark ring; male hind basitarsus slender and parallel-sided. Pupa: 6 gill filaments diverging widely from base, 2 ventral paired filaments short-stalked. Larva: no dorsal reddish-brown markings, no accessory sclerite, mandibular serrations of 2 well-developed teeth without supernumerary serrations (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 193). | S. ruficorne species-group. Monophyletic in COI but exhibits high intraspecific divergence (0-8.42%, mean 4.43%) and splits into 2 OTUs across PTP_ML, ASAP and GMYC in Vietnam/Thailand despite lack of cytogenetic evidence for cryptic species in Thailand (Putt et al. 2023 pp. 11, 18).
SourcesTakaoka et al. 2019, Takaoka & Choochote 2004, Takaoka & Choochote 2005, Mukherjee et al. 2022, Takaoka 2025, Srisuka et al. 2017, Putt et al. 2023
