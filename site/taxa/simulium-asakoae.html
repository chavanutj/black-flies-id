Simulium asakoae Takaoka & Davies, 1995

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Gomphostilbia
Statusvalid
Other names in keysSimulium sp. C
DistributionPeninsular Malaysia, Hong Kong, Thailand, Vietnam (Takaoka et al. 2019) | Thailand (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999; Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000; Takaoka & Choochote 2004). | Peninsular Malaysia, Thailand (Takaoka et al. 2020) | Malaysia (type locality Peninsular Malaysia); Thailand (all 6 regions, low elevations to 2,500 m); Vietnam (Vinh Phuc, Thua Thien Hue, Lam Dong, Nghe An); Hong Kong (China) (Srisuka et al. 2017; Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026)
ThailandThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 55) | Listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 189; previously S. sp. C of Takaoka & Suzuki 1984 per Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999 p. 497); collected at Klang Lum Duan / Huai Luang, Ubon Ratchathani (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999 p. 504), Mae Klang Waterfall, Doi Inthanon NP, Chiang Mai (940 m), Ubon Ratchathani, Nakhon Si Thammarat, Surat Thani and Ranong (Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000 pp. 173-174). | Lampang (Pa Miang, Chae Hom, 1,097 m), Kamphaeng Phet (Tao Dam Waterfall, Klong Lan, 759 m), Phitsanulok (Rangkla, Nakhon Thai, 1,293 m), Chiang Mai (Khun Klang, Doi Inthanon NP, 1,231 m); wide elevation range 196-1,610 m (Takaoka et al. 2020, pp. 141, 147) | Thailand: 1,608 mature larvae (8.3% of total; 2nd most predominant) and most frequent taxon (84.5%, 49/58 sites across all 6 regions, up to 2,500 m on Doi Inthanon) (Srisuka et al. 2017) | Chiang Mai: Ban Pang Dang (932 m) and Ban Pang Bong (981 m), Doi Saket, n=50 females on human bait (Aupalee et al. 2024) | Chiang Mai: Pang-Bong village, Doi Saket (990 m), 23/100 individually screened S. asakoae group flies confirmed as S. asakoae s.str. by multiplex PCR (Aupalee et al. 2026)
ImportanceKnown to bite humans in Thailand (Choochote et al. 2005; Pramual et al. 2016) and incriminated as a natural vector of filariae including an Onchocerca species (Fukuda et al. 2003; Ishii et al. 2008; Takaoka et al. 2003; cited in Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 54). | Natural vector of a filarial species in Thailand (Fukuda et al. 2003, cited in Takaoka et al. 2020, p. 53). | Bites humans and domestic chickens/birds; incriminated as a natural vector of filarial nematodes (including zoonotic Onchocerca spp.) and avian blood protozoa Leucocytozoon spp. and Trypanosoma avium in Thailand; also attracted to humans in northern Vietnam (Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026). In Pang-Bong village, Chiang Mai (Aupalee et al. 2026 Tables 3-5): 6/23 individual S. asakoae s.str. tested positive in head+thorax for Leucocytozoon sp. (haplotype LC-02, 4 flies) and Trypanosoma avium (TP-01, 2 flies); overall S. asakoae group pools (n=20) and individuals (n=100) showed Leucocytozoon (14% ind, 8.5% MIR; incl. first detection of L. sabrazesi and L. schoutedeni), trypanosomatids (10% ind, 4% MIR; T. avium, Leptomonas spiculata, Trypanosomatidae sp.) and filarial nematodes (5% ind, 2.5% MIR; Onchocerca sp. Type I, putative new Onchocerca sp., Filarioidea sp.).
Diagnostic notesSimulium asakoae species-group. Species complex: chromosomal/molecular analyses revealed cryptic diversity in Thai populations (Jitklang et al. 2008, cited in Takaoka et al. 2019). Recorded as Simulium sp. C in Takaoka & Suzuki (1984). | Gomphostilbia (placed in ceylonicum species-group in Takaoka & Choochote 2004; transferred to asakoae species-group in Takaoka et al. 2019). Female: 11-segmented antenna, hind tibia whitish yellow on basal 2/3 without dark subbasal spot, sensory vesicle 0.3x length of 3rd palpal segment. Male: enlarged hind basitarsus, upper eye with 13 horizontal rows of large facets, hind tibia whitish on slightly less than basal 1/2. Pupa: 8 long filaments (>1.9 mm), cocoon with anterodorsal bulge, terminal hooks weakly serrate on outer margin. Larva: simple minute dark setae dorsally on abdominal segments 5-8 (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 pp. 191-192). | S. asakoae species-group (COI subgroup II, sole member). Female: body length 2.4-2.6 mm, mandible with distinct outer teeth, labrum 0.54-0.56x clypeus. Male: upper-eye (large) facets vermilion in 11-12 vertical columns and 13-14 horizontal rows, fore basitarsus 7.1-7.4x greatest width. Pupa: abdominal tergites 1-2 or 1-3 light to medium brown, cocoon with short bulge. Larva: abdominal segments 1-4 greyish/greenish grey, postgenal cleft 1.5-4.0x postgenal bridge, head spots moderately positive (Takaoka et al. 2020). | S. asakoae species-group (71 valid species; Aupalee et al. 2026). Wing centroid size of Thai females 2.64 ± 0.14 mm (range 2.21-2.91 mm); landmark-based wing shape GM achieved 100% (50/50) classification among 7 Thai human-biting species (Aupalee et al. 2024). Morphologically defined Thai 'S. asakoae' females split into 3 (ASAP/PTP) to 4 (GMYC) COI MOTUs (max K2P 7.92%) matching multiple group members; COI alone fails to separate S. asakoae from Vietnamese S. chaudinhense (0.25% min p-distance), S. hongthaii and S. vinhphucense, whereas COI+BZF weakly recovers S. asakoae s.str. as monophyletic (Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024). Multiplex PCR using allele-specific cox1 primers ASK-F1M (463 bp) and ASK-F2M (262 bp) with universal LCO1490/HCO2198 (709 bp) differentiates Thai S. asakoae from non-S. asakoae group members with 100% sensitivity and specificity (Aupalee et al. 2026).
