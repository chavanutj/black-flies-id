Simulium nakhonense Takaoka & Suzuki, 1984

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Simulium
Statusvalid
DistributionMyanmar, Thailand, Vietnam (Takaoka et al. 2019) | Thailand (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999; Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000; Takaoka & Choochote 2004). | Thailand (all 6 regions) (Srisuka et al. 2017) | Vietnam: Nghe An, Pu Mat, 162 m (Putt et al. 2023)
ThailandThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 57) | Listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 190); widespread in northern, northeastern and southern Thailand with S. chaliowae, S. chainarongi, S. angulistylum, S. sheilae, S. dentistylum, S. gombakense, S. grossifilum, S. nobile and S. rudnicki (Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999 pp. 501, 504; Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000 pp. 172-175). | Thailand: 1,451 mature larvae (7.5% of total; 3rd most predominant), 58.6% stream occurrence (34/58 sites); predominant in Central (7.3%), Eastern (7.0%, 85.7% sites), Western (16.1%, 75% sites) and Southern (12.4%, 100% sites) regions; breeds mainly in lowland streams (Srisuka et al. 2017)
Diagnostic notesSimulium striatum species-group (Takaoka et al. 2019). Treated as distinct from S. chiangmaiense (Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 54). | Simulium s.str. (striatum species-group). Adults not separated from S. chiangmaiense (and S. quinquestriatum in female / S. thailandicum in male) in Takaoka & Choochote (2004 pp. 193, 195). Pupa: 10 gill filaments arranged 2+2+2+2+2 with 4-5 dorsal filaments thicker, head integument with round tubercles (angular in S. thailandicum). Larva: dorsal abdominal protuberances, pharate pupal gill 10 filaments in 2+2+2+2+2 arrangement (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 196). | S. striatum species-group. Non-monophyletic in COI with high intraspecific divergence (0-10.15%, mean 5.23%), indicating cryptic diversity; distinguished morphologically from S. quinquestriatum by pupal gill arrangement and from S. taythienense by male upper-eye facets (Putt et al. 2023 pp. 12, 16).
SourcesTakaoka et al. 2019, Takaoka & Kuvangkadilok 1999, Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000, Takaoka & Choochote 2004, Srisuka et al. 2017, Putt et al. 2023
