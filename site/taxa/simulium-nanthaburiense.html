Simulium nanthaburiense Takaoka, Srisuka & Fukuda, 2020

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Gomphostilbia
Statusvalid
DistributionThailand (Nan and Chiang Mai) (Takaoka et al. 2020) | Thailand (Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026)
ThailandType locality: Nam Dan Village, Pua District, Nan Province, 1,157 m, 19°11'10.3"N 101°04'41.7"E, 25-VII-2017; paratype from Huai Mor Nuea, Doi Saket, Chiang Mai, 1,154 m, 2-II-2019 (Takaoka et al. 2020, p. 88) | Thailand: Chiang Mai, Doi Saket (Ban Pang Dang/Ban Pang Bong; Pang-Bong village 990 m, n=5 infected wild females confirmed by COI at 99.39-99.85% identity, haplotypes AK-H1 & AK-H2) (Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026)
ImportanceWild females caught on human bait in Pang-Bong village, Chiang Mai (Aupalee et al. 2026 Tables 3-5): 5/100 individual S. asakoae group flies identified as S. nanthaburiense tested positive in head+thorax for Leucocytozoon sabrazesi (LC-05, n=2), Leucocytozoon sp. (LC-03, n=1), and Onchocerca sp. Type I (FL-03 & FL-04, n=2); potential vector of filarial nematodes and avian Leucocytozoon.
Diagnostic notesS. asakoae species-group (COI subgroup IX; COI almost identical to S. monglaense MF101845). Mature larva unknown. Female: body 2.0 mm, frons:head 1.0:4.3, labrum 0.64x clypeus, mandible with 2 distinct outer teeth, claw tooth 0.43-0.47x claw, sensory vesicle 0.26-0.29x 3rd palp segment, spermatheca 1.6x as long as wide (vs 1.3x in S. huaimorense). Male: brown upper-eye facets in 12 vertical columns and 13-14 horizontal rows (vs 15 x 15 in S. monglaense and 13 columns in S. maehongsonense), ventral plate nearly straight in caudal view. Pupa: stalk of ventral gill pair 0.6-0.9x common basal stalk (vs 1.1-1.2x in S. brinchangense), terminal hook outer/inner margin 1.9-2.1 (vs 3.0-3.6 in S. brinchangense; matches couplet 12a though omitted from printed list) (Takaoka et al. 2020, pp. 88-91, 149). | S. asakoae species-group; morphologically indistinguishable in wild females from S. asakoae; yields 709 bp non-S. asakoae band in multiplex PCR (Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026).
SourcesTakaoka et al. 2020, Aupalee et al. 2024, Aupalee et al. 2026
