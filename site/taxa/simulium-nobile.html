Simulium nobile De Meijere, 1907

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Simulium
Statusvalid
DistributionJava (De Meijere 1907), South Sumatra (Edwards 1934), Peninsular Malaysia and Sabah (Crosskey 1973); recorded from Thailand (Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000; Takaoka & Choochote 2004), though Thai populations were later recognized as S. vanluni (Ya'cob et al. 2017a; Takaoka et al. 2019). | Malaysia: Peninsular Malaysia (Gombak, Selangor) (Low et al. 2015; Takaoka et al. 2016) | Indonesia: Sumatra (West Sumatra, associated with S. kotsuboyui and S. bengkuruense) (Takaoka 2025) | Thailand (Srisuka et al. 2017)
ThailandKa Po Waterfall (40 m), Chumphon Province (8 females, 6 males, 60 pupae, ca. 700 larvae, 5.VIII.1999), Sa Nangmanora Waterfall (50 m), Phangnga Province (6 pupae, 114 larvae, 3.VII.1999), Tone Sai Waterfall (30 m), Phuket Province (7 females, 5 males, 85 pupae, 833 larvae, 2.VIII.1999), southern Thailand (Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000 p. 174); listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 189). Thai records were later re-identified as S. vanluni (Takaoka et al. 2019). | Thailand: 843 mature larvae (4.3% of total), 17.2% stream occurrence; most predominant species in Southern region (31.1%, n=746; 100% of southern sites) (Srisuka et al. 2017)
Diagnostic notesSimulium s.str. (type species of nobile species-group). Female: claw with small subbasal tooth, bare basal radial vein, antenna yellow/tawny with 2 apical segments blackish, simple hairs ventrally on abdominal segment 7. Male: scutum broadly silvery pruinose with transverse inverted-V-shaped black band, narrow parallel-sided ventral plate. Pupa: 6 short slender gill filaments (separating from 3-tubed S. nodosum), tightly woven shoe-shaped cocoon without flap/window, dorsal spine-combs on segment 8, no terminal hooks. Larva: dorsal abdominal protuberances, postgenal cleft reaching hypostomium, pharate pupal gill with 6 filaments (Kuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000 p. 174; Takaoka & Choochote 2004 pp. 193-196). | S. nobile species-group. Concatenated COI+12S+16S+28S genetic distance from S. nodosum is 5.10-5.45% and from S. timorense is 1.00-1.27% (Low et al. 2015 Table 2).
SourcesKuvangkadilok & Takaoka 2000, Takaoka & Choochote 2004, Low et al. 2015, Takaoka et al. 2016, Srisuka et al. 2017, Takaoka 2025
