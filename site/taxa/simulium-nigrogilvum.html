Simulium nigrogilvum Summers, 1911

ClassificationGenus Simulium, subgenus Simulium
Statusvalid
DistributionThailand, Vietnam (Takaoka et al. 2019) | Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004). | Thailand (Tak, northern/other regions) | Vietnam (Putt et al. 2023; Srisuka et al. 2017; Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026)
ThailandThailand (Takaoka et al. 2019, pp. 53, 56) | Listed from Thailand (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 p. 189). | Thailand: 17 mature larvae (0.1% of total), 5.2% stream occurrence (Srisuka et al. 2017) | Tak Province: Umphang (Mae Klong Kee 1,188-1,279 m), n=39 females on human bait (Aupalee et al. 2024)
ImportanceKnown to bite humans in Thailand (Choochote et al. 2005; Pramual et al. 2016) and incriminated as a natural vector of filariae including an Onchocerca species (Fukuda et al. 2003; Ishii et al. 2008; Takaoka et al. 2003; cited in Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 54). | Human-biting species in Thailand; incriminated as a natural vector of filarial and non-filarial nematodes including zoonotic Onchocerca spp. (specifically Onchocerca sp. Type I in Thailand) (Putt et al. 2023; Aupalee et al. 2024, 2026 p. 12).
Diagnostic notesSimulium indicum species-group (Takaoka et al. 2019). One of the first two black fly species recorded from Thailand (Summers 1911, cited in Takaoka et al. 2019, p. 53). | Simulium s.str. (placed in griseifrons species-group in Takaoka & Choochote 2004; transferred to indicum species-group in Takaoka et al. 2019). Female: claw with small subbasal tooth, basal radial vein fully haired, fore basitarsus with thick dorsal hair crest. Male: basal radial vein haired, thick dorsal hair crest on fore basitarsus, parallel-sided hind basitarsus, nearly quadrate ventral plate. Pupa: 6 gill filaments, tightly woven shoe/boot-shaped cocoon without flap/window, no terminal hooks, no spine-combs on abdominal segment 8. Larva: distinct ventral accessory sclerite on last abdominal segment (Takaoka & Choochote 2004 pp. 193-196). | Wing centroid size of Thai females 2.92 ± 0.14 mm (range 2.66-3.16 mm); wing shape GM accuracy 76.92% (30/39; some overlap with S. chamlongi and S. umphangense); separated morphologically from S. umphangense mainly by fore tibia coloration; monophyletic in COI (max K2P 1.23%) and confirmed as distinct species by ASAP, PTP and GMYC (Aupalee et al. 2024).
SourcesTakaoka et al. 2019, Takaoka & Choochote 2004, Srisuka et al. 2017, Putt et al. 2023, Aupalee et al. 2024, Aupalee et al. 2026
