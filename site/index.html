Diptera · Nematocera · Simuliidae

Black Flies of the World

Black flies (Simuliidae) are small, stout-bodied biting flies whose larvae and pupae live in running water. About 2,400 living species are known worldwide (2,422 in the 2026 World Blackflies inventory). Females of some species transmit Onchocerca volvulus (river blindness) and blood parasites of birds such as Leucocytozoon. Identification uses the adult (female and male), the pupa (gill filaments, cocoon) and the mature larva.

This site brings together published dichotomous keys from around the world, from keys to genera to regional species keys, and updates them with later taxonomic work. The species coverage is currently deepest for Thailand and South-East Asia.

Quick start

  1. Decide the life stage: adult female, adult male, pupa or mature larva.
  2. Unknown genus? Start with a key to genera on the keys page. For Simulium from South-East Asia, start with the Thai checklist keys (Takaoka et al. 2019).
  3. Use Step through. At each couplet choose the lead that fits.
  4. Read any ⚠ note on the leads you passed: later work may have added or split species.
  5. Open the species page: compare the diagnostic notes, conflicting characters and figures.
